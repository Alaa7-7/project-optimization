\section{Abstract}
This paper presents a stochastic inverse problem framework for estimating unknown parameters in an advection-diffusion equation. A Bayesian-inspired sampling method is used to recover velocity and diffusion coefficients from noisy observations.

\section{Mathematical Model}

The governing equation is:

\[
\frac{\partial u}{\partial t} + v \frac{\partial u}{\partial x} = D \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x^2}
\]

where $v$ is the advection velocity and $D$ is the diffusion coefficient.

\section{Methodology}

We use a stochastic sampling approach:

\begin{itemize}
\item Random sampling of parameters $(v, D)$
\item Numerical simulation of PDE
\item Cost function based on L2 error
\item Selection of best parameters minimizing cost
\end{itemize}

\section{Results}

True parameters:
\begin{itemize}
\item $v = 0.8$
\item $D = 0.05$
\end{itemize}

Estimated parameters:
\begin{itemize}
\item $v \approx 0.7939$
\item $D \approx 0.0497$
\end{itemize}

The method achieves less than 2\% relative error.

\section{Cost Function}

The optimization minimizes:

\[
J = ||u(v,D) - u_{true}||^2
\]

Observed minimum cost:

\[
J_{min} \approx 0.0415
\]

\section{Conclusion}

The proposed method successfully solves the inverse problem with high accuracy and stable convergence.
